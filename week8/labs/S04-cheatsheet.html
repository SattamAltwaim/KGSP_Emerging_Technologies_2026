Session S04

The sizing loop and the mass budget

Everything you need for the exercise, on one page. There are no answers here — the answers depend on your brief and on the airframe you chose in S03, and working them out is the exercise. Your brief is inside both sheets.

01 Why this is a loop and not a sum

Start hereAnd it follows that
A heavier aircraftneeds more power to stay up
More powermeans more energy for the same endurance
More energymeans a bigger battery — which means a heavier aircraft

To find your takeoff mass you need the battery mass. To find the battery mass you need the power. To find the power you need the takeoff mass. There is no order in which to do these once; you go round.

02 The five steps, in order

1
Guess a takeoff mass. Any sensible number. The loop does not care how good the guess is, only that you are honest about what comes back.
2
Pick a powertrain that can lift it. Use your own T/W target and your rotor count, both from S03, to get the thrust each motor must produce. Then choose motor and propeller from the catalogue.
3
Work out the power to stay up. Hover power from the equation below, plus your payload's power and the avionics.
4
Energy, then battery mass. Power × endurance = energy. Energy ÷ usable specific energy = the mass of battery you must carry.
5
Add it all up, then compare. Structure + propulsion + battery + payload + avionics. Does it match your guess? If not, that answer is your next guess.
Keep every pass, including the ones that did not work. A sheet showing one pass is a guess written down neatly. The convergence — or the runaway — is the evidence you did this at all. And do not round between passes: carry the mass forward as it came out, or the loop will refuse to settle.

Air density, 1.225 kg/m³

FM
Figure of merit, 0.65

Motor + ESC efficiency, 0.85
Energy, then battery mass
E = Ptotal × t      mbatt = E ⁄ (usable Wh/kg)
t
Endurance in hours. 25 minutes is 0.417 h
usable
80% of the stated Wh/kg. You do not fly a pack flat
Two things the equation is telling you. Power rises with mass to the power 1.5 — add 20% of mass and pay about 33% more power. And it falls with √A — that is S03's disc loading, and the propeller you chose is the largest single lever you have here.
On an X8, your disc area is four propellers' worth, not eight — momentum theory sees the disc, not the motor. The coaxial interference loss comes off on top of that.

Read the units twice. The thrust column is per motor, and four motors on a quadcopter means four times the mass and four times the power. A team whose aircraft weighs 400 g has divided by four somewhere. Usable energy is 80% of stated, on every row.
Li-ion carries far more energy per kilogram and delivers far less peak current. That is a real trade, not a free upgrade. If you pick B3 or B4 for a heavy aircraft, say what you checked about the discharge rate — the moment it matters is takeoff, not cruise.

05 If your loop does not close

It happens, and it is not a mistake. Every extra gram of battery costs more power than the energy it carries buys back, and past a certain requirement no number of passes brings it back. There are three legitimate responses.

1
Grow the disc. Power falls with √A. Bigger propellers, or more of them. Costs mass and folded size, but it attacks the problem at its source — this is the answer the physics likes.
2
Change the chemistry. Li-ion carries roughly 50% more energy per kilogram than LiPo, and will not give you the burst current a heavy aircraft wants at takeoff. Say which you traded.
3
Go back to the requirement. The endurance you were given may not be purchasable in this configuration at this budget. Name the requirement, say what it costs, and propose the number that does close.
Option 3 is not giving up. A team that shows exactly what an extra ten minutes of endurance costs has done engineering. A team that quietly writes down a smaller battery — or a smaller payload — has not.

06 The mass budget

RowWhat goes in it
StructureFrame, arms, landing gear, wiring, fasteners, and any guards
PropulsionMotors and ESCs and propellers — one set per rotor
BatteryFrom your loop. Then check you can actually buy that mass in whole packs
PayloadOff the brief. The brief also gives its power, which is not a mass row but decides how much battery is
AvionicsFlight controller, GNSS, radio, and the wiring between them
They must sum to the takeoff mass you are claiming. If they do not, saying which number is wrong is part of the answer — and a better answer than quietly adjusting one until it balances. The Why column is where component selection is actually assessed: one line per item, and "it was in the middle" is not a line.

07 Three ways teams lose marks

Payload power forgotten

  • The commonest miss in the whole course, flagged in three sessions.
  • Fix: the brief gives a mass and a wattage. Both go in — one in the budget, one in the energy.

One pass, written neatly

  • A single row is a guess, not a loop, and it is marked as one.
  • Fix: keep every pass, including the ones heading the wrong way.

An endurance your own budget does not support

  • Promising 25 minutes in one section and sizing for 15 in another is the gap the review asks about.
  • Fix: write the endurance your sizing supports next to the one your brief demands. A gap is not a failure. An unexamined gap is.

08 Before you hand it in

Check these four, in this order. Payload power is in the energy. Thrust is per motor, not total. Usable energy is 80% of stated. Your rows sum to your claimed takeoff mass. Four checks, two minutes, and they catch most of what is lost above.

You will be asked these four at the design review

  1. Your takeoff mass, and the heaviest single line in your budget
  2. The endurance your sizing supports, against the one your brief demands
  3. Whether S03's airframe survived — and if not, what you changed
  4. The number you are least confident in

Question 4 has followed you all week. In S01 it was what would change your mind; in S02, what evidence would change your mind; here it is which of your own numbers do you distrust. Naming one is the answer. Claiming there isn't one is not.
