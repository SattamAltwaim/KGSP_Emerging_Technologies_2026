Brief AShallow-Reef Photographic SurveyCoastal and marine · survey

Operator KAUST Coastal & Marine Resources Core Lab

Reef flats along the Red Sea coast are bleaching. The lab needs a photographic record precise enough and repeatable enough to measure how far the bleaching has spread between one visit and the next — which means the same lines, at the same resolution, every time.

Boat-based survey does not do this. It is slow, it is expensive in ship time, and a hull cannot get over the shallowest flats, which are exactly the parts changing fastest. They want an aerial system they can redeploy every quarter for five years, and they want to know before they buy it whether one aircraft can do the job in the time available.

What you must deliver

A continuous photographic strip of the coastal corridor, overlapping enough to be stitched into a single orthomosaic, at a resolution fine enough to identify individual coral heads.

The numbers

Survey corridor
12 km of coastline × 400 m offshore — 4.8 km²
Ground sample distance
≤ 2 cm per pixel, across the whole corridor, not just at the centre
Image overlap
70% forward, 60% side
Survey window
The full 12 km within one working day, sea state permitting
Repeatability
The same flight lines, quarterly, for 5 years
Data
Every image geotagged. Recorded onboard; no live downlink needed

Where you will be operating

  • There is no runway, no catapult and no prepared surface. Launch and recovery is from an open beach or the aft deck of a 9 m survey vessel — roughly 3 × 4 m of clear space, with rigging above it.
  • Onshore wind 4–9 m/s through the day, gusting higher after mid-afternoon
  • Air temperature 30–40 °C, salt spray throughout, strong glare off the water
  • Most of every sortie is spent over water, out to 400 m from shore. There is nowhere to put an aircraft down between the beach and the far edge of the corridor.

What the aircraft must carry

  • A down-looking camera capable of the required GSD, on a stabilised mount
  • Allow 400 g for camera plus mount, drawing 8 W in flight
  • Onboard recording. No live video link is required, but position must be logged with each frame or the imagery cannot be stitched.

What you cannot change

  • Total system cost ≤ $8,000 per aircraft
  • A two-person crew, both of whom are also running the boat. Nobody is free to do nothing but watch the aircraft.
  • The whole system travels in one vehicle with the crew and their dive gear
  • A vehicle lost over water is a total loss. There is no recovery. The lab has asked what happens if the aircraft fails mid-corridor and expects a real answer, not "it returns to launch".

Yours to decide, and to defend

Cruise speed. Survey altitude. How many passes, and in which direction relative to the coast. How many sorties, and whether one aircraft or more than one. Battery chemistry and capacity. What happens on a failure over water.

Brief BDesalination Plant Asset InspectionPlant integrity · close-range inspection

Operator Operations & Integrity, Red Sea desalination facility

Structural inspection of the intake works, the pipe racks and the exhaust stack currently needs rope-access teams and a scaffold permit. It is slow, it is the single highest-risk routine activity on the site, and it forces partial shutdowns that cost more than the inspection does.

The integrity group wants imagery good enough to grade corrosion — to decide whether a given weld or flange is fine, watch-listed, or needs intervention — gathered without putting a person on a rope. What they are buying is close-range imagery of things that are difficult and dangerous to stand next to.

What you must deliver

Inspection-grade imagery of vertical and overhead structures, with enough positional context that a finding can be located again on the next visit.

The numbers

Site footprint
200 × 150 m
Tallest asset
45 m exhaust stack
Standoff from structure
3–5 m — closer is unsafe, further loses detail
Position hold while capturing
Within ± 0.5 m
Useful time on station
≥ 20 minutes per sortie, not counting transit or reserve
Coverage per campaign
14 assets, inspected across two working days

Where you will be operating

  • Dense steel and concrete in every direction. Expect GPS shadowing and multipath between the pipe racks, and magnetic interference near the stack. A position estimate that is good on the apron may be useless twenty metres in.
  • Wind funnels between the structures — gusts to 8 m/s at height on a day that is calm at ground level
  • Ambient 35–45 °C, with hot plumes rising above the process equipment
  • The plant keeps running while you work. Flight over live process areas is prohibited. The aircraft works from the perimeter and the access corridors, so it is often at the limit of its standoff rather than the middle of it.

What the aircraft must carry

  • A high-resolution visual camera and a thermal sensor
  • Both on a mount that can look upward as well as down. The underside of overhead pipe runs is the hardest part of this job, and the part rope access does worst.
  • Allow 600 g for sensors plus mount, drawing 14 W
  • A live view to the inspector, who decides where to look next while the aircraft is in the air. This is not a survey that can be planned in advance and reviewed afterwards.

What you cannot change

  • Total system cost ≤ $12,000 per aircraft
  • One operator, plus one plant escort who is not a pilot and cannot be given any task requiring flying skill
  • The aircraft must reach 45 m and hold there in gusts
  • It must behave predictably when GPS degrades. The operator has been shown a video of an aircraft drifting into a structure and will ask you directly what yours does.

Yours to decide, and to defend

How you hold position where GPS is unreliable. Whether one aircraft covers all 14 assets or you propose something else. What redundancy you buy, and what you pay for it. Which sensors, and whether they fly together or on separate passes.

Brief CDesert Search for an Overdue VehicleCivil defence · search and rescue

Operator Regional civil defence, in agreement with campus security

Vehicles travelling between remote sites occasionally fail to arrive. A ground search of the sector takes a full day with several teams, and in summer the survivable window for a person without shade or water is far shorter than that.

What civil defence wants is not a better map. It is time: something that can be in the air within minutes of arriving, sweep the sector far faster than vehicles can, and then stay over the target so ground teams drive to a live marker instead of a grid reference.

What you must deliver

Search a defined sector, locate a vehicle-sized target, and then remain over it.

The numbers

Search sector
8 × 5 km — 40 km²
Target
A vehicle, roughly 4 × 2 m, possibly under a tarpaulin
First full sweep
Complete within 90 minutes of arriving on scene
Detection resolution
≤ 10 cm per pixel by day; thermal at night
On station after locating
≥ 15 minutes overhead, holding, while ground teams close in
Live downlink
Position and video to the ground team throughout, to 8 km

Where you will be operating

  • Open desert. No runway, no power, no shade, no infrastructure of any kind.
  • Launch and recovery from the bed or roof of a pickup, on unprepared ground, with whatever the team carried in
  • 40–48 °C by day; afternoon wind to 12 m/s with blowing dust
  • Terrain relief up to 60 m across the sector, so line of sight to the ground station is not guaranteed everywhere
  • Called out at any hour, including at night

What the aircraft must carry

  • A combined visual and thermal sensor on a stabilised mount
  • Allow 700 g for sensors plus mount, drawing 18 W
  • Live video and position downlink to a ground station up to 8 km away

What you cannot change

  • Total system cost ≤ $25,000
  • A three-person team arriving in one vehicle and setting up in the open
  • Ready to launch within 15 minutes of arriving on scene
  • Must operate at 40 °C+ without thermal shutdown
  • Losing the aircraft is acceptable if the target is found. Failing to find the target is not. Design accordingly — this constraint is doing more work than it looks.

Yours to decide, and to defend

Search pattern, altitude and sweep speed. Which sensor and which lens — the resolution requirement and the height you are allowed to fly at are not independent. Whether searching and holding are done by the same aircraft. How the 90-minute sweep sits against the endurance you claim.

This brief contains a genuine conflict between two of its own requirements. Find it, name it in your report, and say what you did about it. A brief that appears to have no conflict has been read too quickly.

01 Three requirements. Only three. Core
